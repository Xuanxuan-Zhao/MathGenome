D11 MEASURE, INTEGRATION AND REAL ANALYSIS: CANONICAL CONVENTIONS
Adopted for authoring on 2026-11-02; references to chapter numbers are mathematical
dependencies, not prescribed Lean goals or a scheduler. All statements and proofs
remain subject to independent checking. The 23-family working scope controls.

FOUNDATIONS AND SCOPE
F_SET: Classical logic, sets, functions, countable families, quotients, choice,
well-ordering and Zorn. State where choice matters (Vitali sets, Hahn--Banach).
F_SCALAR: The ordered complete real field, complex field, elementary sequences,
series and finite sums; no measure construction or convergence theorem is assumed.
F_LINEAR: Finite-dimensional algebra, determinants and Euclidean geometry, and
the normed-space vocabulary. Hilbert projection and the particular representation
theorems needed in this volume are proved in the volume, not silently assumed.
F_CALCULUS: Established single-variable calculus and the C1 inverse-function/
chain-rule machinery from D03/D04; the Lebesgue Jacobian formula is a D11 result,
not a prerequisite disguised as background. State any Riemann bridge explicitly.
F_TOPO: The published D10 general topology: compactness without implicit Hausdorff,
normality without implicit T1, locally compact Hausdorff spaces, Urysohn/Tietze,
partitions of unity under their stated hypotheses, Baire and metric completeness.
Use exact published hypotheses. General functional analysis, Fourier/distribution
theory, probability/ergodicity and advanced geometric measure theory remain routed.
The notes are self-contained relative to these explicit prior foundations; they
are not claimed to reconstruct the real numbers or all of set theory.

SPACES, MEASURES AND GENERATION
C01. A measurable space is (X,Sigma); a positive measure takes values in [0,infinity],
is zero on the empty set and is countably additive. Infinite values are allowed.
Empty spaces, zero measures and empty families remain allowed unless excluded.
C02. Finite means mu(X)<infinity. Sigma-finite means a countable measurable cover
by finite-measure sets. Semifinite and finite on compact sets are distinct notions.
Never add sigma-finiteness to an otherwise valid arbitrary-measure theorem merely
to simplify a later theorem; retain useful stronger source versions when proved.
C03. A null set in Sigma has measure zero. On an incomplete space an arbitrary
subset of a measurable null set need not belong to Sigma. The completion has its
own sigma-algebra and extended measure; distinguish these structures explicitly.
C04. For abstract spaces measures are defined on Sigma. A Borel measure is on the
Borel sigma-algebra. Lebesgue measure on R^n means its completed Borel volume
measure; write the Borel restriction when that smaller sigma-algebra is intended.
C05. Premeasure extension exists on the generated sigma-algebra. Sigma-finiteness
is a sufficient uniqueness hypothesis, not an automatic consequence of extension.
Pi-lambda, set monotone-class and functional monotone-class tools have separate
precise conclusions and are proved before uses in uniqueness or products.
C06. Caratheodory measurability tests all subsets. A metric outer measure is
additive on positively separated sets; prove Borel measurability from that fact.
Do not use Lebesgue regularity before the Euclidean measure is constructed.

FUNCTIONS, ALMOST EVERYWHERE AND INTEGRATION
C07. A scalar measurable function is finite-valued unless the codomain [0,infinity]
or the extended reals is displayed. For extended signed values exclude indeterminate
infinity-infinity operations explicitly rather than treating them as field arithmetic.
C08. f=g almost everywhere means equality outside a measurable null exceptional
set (or its completed-measure equivalent). Distinguish representative-level
statements from quotient-level statements, and a.e. from everywhere equality.
C09. On incomplete spaces an a.e. modification need only be measurable relative
to the completion. State measurable representatives where required. Scalar Borel
measurability, a.e. measurability and general Banach strong measurability are not
interchanged; only scalar integration is adopted as the primary integral here.
C10. Nonnegative integration has values in [0,infinity], with 0*infinity=0 in
simple-function sums. Establish monotone convergence and Fatou before subtracting
integrals. A signed/complex scalar function is integrable iff integral |f|<infinity.
C11. A Lebesgue integral of a signed or complex function in these notes is used
as a linear functional on integrable functions. If a library assigns a totalized
value to nonintegrable inputs, prove integrability before transporting a formula.
C12. Complex integrals use real and imaginary parts, with integral absolute-value
bounds. Absolute integrability justifies Fubini; conditionally convergent improper
integrals are not automatically Lebesgue integrals on the whole unbounded domain.
C13. Dominated convergence requires an integrable dominator and a.e. convergence;
the exceptional set may be made common for a countable family. Parameter and
limit interchanges have their measurability and domination hypotheses displayed.

PRODUCTS, TRANSPORT AND EUCLIDEAN FORMULAS
C14. The product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles. State
sigma-finiteness in the canonical product/Tonelli/Fubini theorem. Completion of
the product is a further operation; sections of a completed measurable set are
asserted measurable a.e. with respect to the appropriate completed factors.
C15. Pushforward u_*mu is defined for a measurable map and requires no finiteness.
Nonnegative transport and integrable transport are different statements. Equality
of densities requires an explicit RN/integrability justification.
C16. The basic nonlinear Jacobian theorem is for a C1 diffeomorphism between open
subsets of R^n, with absolute determinant and the corresponding measurable-set
and nonnegative/integrable formulations. Prove its extension to completed measures.
Keep injectivity and differentiability requirements of each more general variant.
Do not silently replace a general Jacobian formula by a linear special case.
C17. Use the Euclidean norm and the standard determinant normalization. Polar
coordinates, one-dimensional monotone-AC substitution and rectifiable-curve
integration require their own domains, endpoint and multiplicity hypotheses.

Lp, DENSITY AND CONVERGENCE
C18. 1<=p<infinity unless another exponent range is explicitly adopted. Lp(mu)
is the normed space of a.e. classes of measurable scalar functions with finite
p-integral. L-infinity is the ordinary a.e. quotient with essential-supremum norm.
The zero-measure case is handled through the quotient, not pointwise norms.
C19. Cohn's local-null L-infinity convention on arbitrary non-sigma-finite spaces
is recorded separately. It is not identified with ordinary a.e. L-infinity by
notation alone. Prove the specific bridge, in particular the sigma-finite case.
C20. Preserve Holder/Minkowski endpoint hypotheses. Finite-measure inclusions and
continuous/smooth density are not extended automatically to p=infinity. Local
integrability means integrability on compact/relatively compact regions as stated.
C21. Convergence in measure means mu{|f_n-f|>epsilon}->0 for every epsilon>0;
local convergence in measure is a distinct notion on infinite spaces. Egorov's
theorem requires a finite-measure domain; Lusin requires the actual regularity
and finiteness hypotheses used in its proof.
C22. Name uniform integral control explicitly: UI_small is uniform absolute
continuity on small-measure measurable sets; UI_tail is the large-|f| integral
tail criterion; L1 boundedness and finite-measure spatial tail control are
separate predicates. State and prove each equivalence under exact hypotheses.
C23. Folland's |integral_E f| formulation and Royden's integral_E |f| formulation
are not identical definitions. Any scalar comparison must supply its decomposition
argument, including real/imaginary parts for complex functions. On infinite
domains small-set control alone does not prevent mass escaping to infinity.
C24. Vitali's canonical finite-p Cauchy/convergence criterion states convergence
in measure, UI of |f_n|^p and the required finite-measure spatial tail property.
Preserve the distinct finite-measure and arbitrary-space Royden variants; do not
drop a source's hypothesis that the proposed limiting function is integrable.

BV, STIELTJES AND DIFFERENTIATION
C25. On a compact interval AC implies BV, and BV implies a.e. differentiability;
BV and everywhere differentiability are incomparable. BV does not imply AC.
Use classical partition variation, allowing degenerate intervals with variation 0.
C26. Normalize nondecreasing Stieltjes generators as right-continuous; the measure
of (a,b] is F(b)-F(a). The point mass at x is F(x)-F(x-). Fix the additive constant
when claiming uniqueness of the generator; account for endpoints on bounded
intervals. Signed/complex BV correspondence uses the same interval convention.
C27. AC on [a,b] is equivalent to recovery by an L1 derivative via the Lebesgue
FTC, not via an assumed identity. Prove the differentiation theorem and required
covering/maximal estimates before invoking them. The route may use later proved
RN decomposition if the resulting dependency graph is explicitly acyclic.
C28. Lebesgue differentiation uses centered Euclidean balls (or an explicitly
proved equivalent averaging family), local integrability and the actual a.e.
conclusion. Weak-(1,1) constants need not be sharp but must be correctly proved.

SIGNED MEASURES, RN AND DUALITY
C29. A general signed measure is allowed one infinite sign only. Develop finite
signed/complex measures and finite total variation first. Complex measures here
are countably additive finite complex-valued measures with finite variation.
C30. Hahn decomposition, Jordan decomposition, variation, absolute continuity
and mutual singularity are measure statements, not representative-level signs.
State a common dominating positive measure before density identifications.
C31. The canonical positive RN theorem is sigma-finite nu absolutely continuous
with respect to sigma-finite mu; densities are nonnegative measurable and unique
mu-a.e. Give the finite signed/complex target version and its exact reference-
measure hypotheses separately. Do not promote existence to pointwise uniqueness.
C32. Organize the positive RN proof by Hahn decomposition/supremal admissible
densities, not by invoking Lp duality that later depends on RN. Finite complex
Lebesgue decomposition relative to an arbitrary positive reference measure has
a direct maximal-null-set proof; it does not imply an unrestricted RN density.
A finite target does NOT supply a sigma-finite support for the reference measure
(even a one-point infinite reference measure and a unit point mass refute that
claim). Preserve Axler's useful arbitrary-reference decomposition exactly, and
state separate hypotheses before representing its absolutely continuous part
as a density. Do not discard stronger valid source ranges by convention.
C33. Lp duality for 1<p<infinity is retained for arbitrary positive measure when
the adopted proof establishes it. The p=1 identification with ordinary L-infinity
uses sigma-finiteness in the canonical endpoint theorem. The dual of L-infinity
is not generally L1. Complex dual pairings and the linear argument are explicit.
C34. Minimal Hilbert material (L2 completeness, projection, Cauchy-Schwarz and
Riesz representation) is proved as needed; no general Banach/Hilbert theorem is
an undeclared foundation. General weak compactness and interpolation remain routed.

TOPOLOGICAL MEASURES AND REPRESENTATION
C35. Separate compact-finiteness, local finiteness, outer regularity on Borel
sets, inner regularity on opens, inner regularity on all Borel sets, and tightness.
No arbitrary metric-space finite Borel measure is declared tight without the
actual separability/completeness or other hypotheses supplying compact approximation.
C36. In this volume a Radon measure on LCH Hausdorff X is a Borel positive measure
finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets and inner regular on opens.
Full regularity adds compact inner approximation on all Borel sets. State when
sigma-compactness or finite-measure restrictions upgrade one form to another.
Compare Folland/Royden/Cohn by these predicates rather than just the word regular.
C37. C_c(X) consists of continuous functions of genuinely compact support; C_0(X)
means continuous functions vanishing at infinity. A bounded closed ball need not
be compact in a general metric space. Do not adopt Stein Ch6 Problem5's stronger
bounded-support claim as a general theorem; its identified counterexample is noted.
C38. Positive-functionals-on-C_c Riesz--Markov produces the exact Radon Borel
measure. Bounded functionals on C_0 correspond to finite regular signed/complex
measures with the proper total-variation norm. Establish cutoff/partition and
extension steps needed by the actual proof rather than citing the conclusion.
C39. Left Haar measure on an LCH Hausdorff group is nonzero, left invariant and
Radon, unique up to a positive scalar. Right invariance is not assumed; finite
total mass occurs in the compact case. Compact-group normalization requires
nonzero total mass. Give the selected existence and uniqueness proof in full.
C40. Hausdorff outer measure uses diameter covers with coefficient one, and
H^s=lim_{delta down to0} H^s_delta. At s=0 each nonempty covering set costs one,
including singletons. H^0 is counting measure; dim_H(empty) is defined explicitly
(choose 0). Similarity/Lipschitz formulas include s=0 and zero-Lipschitz cases
separately. No general local or sigma-finiteness is inferred from the construction.
C41. Daniell--Stone uses a vector lattice of real functions satisfying the Stone
truncation condition, a positive linear functional, and continuity from above at
zero. Keep the generated sigma-ring uniqueness statement (Cohn7.7.4) distinct
from a sigma-algebra uniqueness claim. State the exact extension/coverage needed
before deriving a sigma-algebra or finite-mass specialization.
