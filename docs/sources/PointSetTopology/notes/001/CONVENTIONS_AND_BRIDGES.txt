D10 — CANONICAL CONVENTIONS, DEDUPLICATION AND PROOF INTERFACES
2026-11-02

1. One mathematical object, several source presentations
Topologies, bases/subbases, neighborhoods and closure operators are related by
proved equivalences in01; they are not separate competing primitive structures.
Initial constructions (products/subspaces) and final constructions (sums/quotients)
receive their actual universal properties in03. A set bijection is not silently
called a homeomorphism.

2. Separation
Regular and normal do not include T1; T3=regular+T1, T4=normal+T1. Compact does not
include Hausdorff. Tychonoff means completely regular+Hausdorff. Munkres/Willard/
Kelley/Steen-Seebach do not share every terminology convention. Concrete
hypotheses, rather than a matching T-number, control the translation.

3. Convergence and filter order
Ordinary convergence filters are proper. A filter converges when it contains the
neighborhood filter. Moore-Smith subnets use monotone cofinal index maps;04 proves
the interface to eventual-cofinal maps used by Kelley. Sequence/closure/continuity
tests use their stated first-countable or metric hypotheses. General compactness
uses open covers/FIP/nets/ultrafilters as proved, not a universal sequence test.
Lean's filter-order notation must be checked explicitly at formalization.

4. Metric/uniform interfaces
Completeness belongs to a specified metric/uniformity. Complete metrizability is
a topology-level existence property. A pseudometric completion first identifies
zero-distance points. Chapter12 proves arbitrary uniform pseudometric
representation, countable-base metrization and separated uniform completion,
with the dense uniformly continuous extension property and uniqueness.
The empty uniform space has the unique degenerate entourage family; ordinary
proper convergence filters on the empty set do not exist.

5. Compactification/properness
One-point construction handles compact and empty spaces, distinguishing a compact
extension from a dense compactification. Stone-Cech is for Tychonoff spaces with
compact Hausdorff targets. Proper is compact-preimage proper. The equivalence
with closed maps having compact fibres is used only with the theorem's LCH
Hausdorff target hypotheses.

6. Function-space interfaces
Pointwise, uniform, compact convergence and compact-open are distinct until
proved equal in the specified setting. Joint evaluation/composition includes
local compactness conditions;18 gives evaluation failure on Q.
Ascoli sufficiency/necessity scopes and metric-target relative compactness are
explicit. Pointwise boundedness replaces pointwise compactness only in the
finite-dimensional real/complex target cases actually proved.

7. Approximation and fixed points
The real closed algebra becomes a lattice by a proved polynomial absolute-value
approximation, avoiding circular use of Stone-Weierstrass. Complex algebras need
conjugation invariance. C0 approximation uses the one-point extension and a
nowhere-vanishing hypothesis. Banach includes quantitative contraction estimates;
Brouwer uses a proved triangulation/Sperner argument and applies to every
nonempty compact convex finite-dimensional set, including lower-dimensional sets.

8. Examples and alternate representations
An example is identified by its set, topology and proved properties. The
topologist's sine closure, a graph with just one added point, and different
double-origin constructions are not interchanged by name. STE chart entries
are checked in the canonical conventions. The Cantor product, ternary compact
set, characterization and continuous-surjection model have explicit bridges.

9. Deduplication
Use one substantive result and its actual specializations, not one theorem per
textbook notation. Keep useful corollaries, bridges and counterexamples. The atlas
reuses existing proof labels rather than repeating those proofs. No optional
maximal generalization is required merely for publication; all adopted source
results retain their complete stated conditions and conclusions.
